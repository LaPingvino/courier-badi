# Courier Badi — design & engineering notes

This document records a few non-obvious design decisions, mainly so reviewers
(including Google Fonts onboarding) don't have to reverse-engineer them.

## Monospace model

Every glyph advances **1228 units** (UPM 2048). The font is strictly
monospaced: `post.isFixedPitch` is set, the PANOSE proportion byte is 9
(monospaced), and `hhea.numberOfHMetrics` is 1 (all glyphs share one width — a
valid and compact choice for a monospaced font; see
fonttools/fonttools#3014).

### Combining marks are zero-width overlays

Spacing/base glyphs advance 1228; **combining marks (Unicode categories Mn/Me)
advance 0**. They are drawn as overlays positioned to sit over the *preceding*
base cell, which is the standard model for combining marks and lets sequences
like `i` + U+0301 stack correctly instead of consuming a second cell.

The common accented letters used for Bahá'í and European orthographies
(á í ú ñ …) are **precomposed** single glyphs at the normal 1228 width, so the
zero-width marks matter only for arbitrary base+mark combinations.

The Arabic tashkil **isolated presentation forms** in U+FE70–U+FE7F are *not*
combining marks — they are standalone spacing forms and keep the 1228 width and
are excluded from the GDEF mark class.

> Note: this zero-width model was introduced deliberately. If a fontbakery run
> or a reviewer flags mark widths, this is the intended design, not an artifact.

### Mark positioning (GPOS)

Until the Hebrew, the font had **no GPOS**: the marks were placed by the
shaper's *fallback* mark positioning, which HarfBuzz applies only to fonts
without a GPOS table. Hebrew niqqud needs real per-letter positioning, so the
font now has a GPOS `mark`/`mkmk` feature, generated by ufo2ft from UFO
anchors. Because HarfBuzz then switches its fallback off for **every** script,
the other scripts' marks carry anchors too:

- `scripts/make-mark-anchors.py` writes HarfBuzz's fallback rule down as
  anchors: the mark's ink centred on the cell, its ink bottom `UPM/16` = 128
  above the base's ink top (below: 128 under the ink bottom; attached classes:
  no gap), stacked marks another 128 out. The fallback also clamps (an above
  mark that would move down moves only half way; a below mark is never moved
  up), which depends on where the mark is drawn; so the marks of each class
  are grouped by their drawn edge (within 40 units; 42 groups, most of them
  Arabic, whose marks are drawn at many heights), each group has its own
  anchor name (`aboveArab3`, `belowLGC1`, …), and each base's anchor for a
  group has the clamp folded in. Classes come from the Unicode combining
  classes, with HarfBuzz's Arabic re-categorisation. It runs on every master
  in `make masters` (anchors follow each master's ink; the groups are made on
  the Regular so all masters share the anchor names). **Arabic is the
  exception since 1.102**: its marks sit `ARAB_GAP` = 50 from the letter's
  ink (24 on another mark), with no clamps, a below mark measured under its
  own footprint only; see "Arabic in the screenplay line".
- `scripts/check-mark-parity.py fonts/ttf/*.ttf` shapes every base × mark pair
  (and two-mark stacks) of Latin, Greek, Cyrillic and Arabic with the GPOS and
  with the GPOS stripped (= the old fallback) and reports the differences. At
  1.100: Arabic within 19 units everywhere (isolated and medial forms) --
  since 1.102 Arabic differs from the fallback on purpose (closer marks), so
  its lines of the report are expected to differ; Latin/Greek/Cyrillic single marks within ~46 units (the grouping
  tolerance, on clamped pairs); two-mark stacks up to ~110 units (the second
  mark of dot-below + macron-below, circumflex + acute), because mark-to-mark
  anchors stack on the first mark's ink, which is not quite the fallback's
  bookkeeping. A mark on a base of the other script family (an Arabic mark on
  a Latin letter) is not positioned.
- Hebrew uses its own anchor classes (`top`, `bottom`, `center`, `shindot`,
  `sindot`, `holam`, …), taken from the donor; see "Hebrew" below.
- The GDEF glyph classes are generated with the Arabic features
  (`scripts/arabic-features`): marks are the Mn/Me codepoints, **bases are the
  glyphs that carry an attaching anchor**. The base class is essential: with a
  GDEF table in `features.fea`, ufo2ft attaches marks only to glyphs listed as
  bases, so without it the anchors would silently produce no GPOS.

## Arabic shaping

Arabic OpenType features are **generated**, not hand-written, by
`scripts/arabic-features` (Go). See its package doc for details. In short:

- `init` / `medi` / `fina` single substitutions are emitted for every base
  letter whose positional-form glyph exists in the UFO — either the Unicode
  presentation form (from the Unicode Character Database) or the
  `<base>.init/.medi/.fina` naming convention used for extended letters that
  have no Unicode presentation form (e.g. Pashto U+0681, U+0685).
- The mandatory **lam-alef** ligatures are matched against the *positional*
  forms (`lam.init`/`lam.medi` + `alef.fina`), because HarfBuzz applies
  init/medi/fina **before** `rlig`; rules written against the base letters
  never match and the ligature silently fails to form.
- The `GDEF` mark class is built from Unicode categories (Mn/Me), excluding the
  U+FE70–FE7F isolated forms noted above.

Regenerate after adding/removing Arabic glyphs:

```
make arabic-features         # rewrite the managed block in features.fea
make arabic-features-check   # verify it is up to date (CI-friendly)
```

The generated block lives between markers in
`sources/CourierBadi-Regular.ufo/features.fea`; hand-written features outside
the markers (see below) are preserved.

## Arabic in the screenplay line

A screenplay is single-spaced: 12 pt type, six lines to the inch, so every
line has one em (2048 units; Lexington's PDF puts the baseline 1628 under the
line's top and 399 above its bottom). The Latin fits it. The Arabic as merged
from No Name Fixed did not: its letters ran from -1024 (final yeh) to 1845,
its vowel marks were 530-680 units tall and stood 128 off the letter, so a
vowelled line reached ~2600 up and ~1700 down and consecutive cue,
parenthetical and dialogue lines ran into each other.

`scripts/fit-arabic.py` (run once on the Regular; the glyphs are tagged
`org.courierbadi.arabic` = fitted, so a second run changes nothing; `make
masters` carries it to the other masters) brings the Arabic into the line
and keeps the advance and every join:

- One vertical map for all Arabic letter glyphs, so the joins (all on the
  same baseline stroke, 225-369) still meet: the identity in the body
  (225-800: baseline stroke, bowls, teeth), ascenders shortened to 0.32 of
  their height above it (alef 1477 → ~1060, lam 1593 → ~1100), descenders
  kept at 0.75 for the first 400 units under the body (reh, zain, waw and
  noon still clearly go below: reh is not dal) and flattened to 0.2 beyond
  (the deep bowls of jeem, ain, meem, final yeh). Each on-curve point then
  grows back out by half of what its stroke lost, the stroke's thickness
  measured vertically into the ink (a run longer than 230 is a stem's end,
  which just moves); handles follow their point. Bowls and tails keep
  ~140-165 of their 145-170; steep diagonals in a compressed zone (the
  lam-alef's alef, keheh's bar) come out thinner. x is untouched.
- Dots, hamzas, the wasla and kaf's sign (separate contours that are not
  counters, small or wholly outside the body) move rigidly: dot clusters
  scaled 0.75 (271 → 203, still heavier than the stroke), a sign drawn as a
  stroke 0.52 with its stroke grown back; a cluster then comes as close as
  110 units to the ink under (over) it, looking 150 units to either side, so
  teh's dots come down between its horns but zain's dot stays above the
  reh's head, and is pushed away again where the map brought it closer than
  min(its old gap, 110). The Bold grows every contour 34 units, so 110 in
  the Regular is ~40 in the Bold: 90 dot clusters of 826 come within 40
  units of their letter in the Bold (107 in 1.101), 41 in the Regular (22),
  all in extended letters or ones that were that tight before.
- The spacing hamzas (U+0621, U+0674, U+FE80) are left as they were.
- Marks are scaled 0.42 about their centre with the stroke grown back to 124
  (a fatha is now ~290 units tall, about the Latin acute), and moved by the
  same map. The anchors (above) are made from this ink.

Measured with HarfBuzz on the Regular (every letter of U+0621-064A, four
positions, with each mark; the line's body is 1628 / −399): bare letters
span −495 … 1402 (was −1024 … 1845; the −495 is the dots under final
yeh, the deepest common case, so علي reaches 95 under the line's bottom).
With one above mark 37 of 192 forms pass 1600 (letters with a hamza or
three dots above, hamza-alef, teh marbuta; ≈1640-1740, the Latin's É is
1685); a kasra takes 77 of 192 forms under −370 (beh, jeem, ain, the
yehs: −420 … −830). Shadda + vowel on a tall letter (the lam of اللّه)
reaches ≈1780-2010. So two vowelled lines can still touch where a stacked
mark over a tall letter falls exactly under a kasra below a deep letter
(in a test of six fully vowelled lines, two pairs still touch, by up to
258 units, where before all six did, by up to 1670); Accolade's Arabic
sample's cue, parenthetical and dialogue lines no longer touch.

## Stylistic sets

- **ss01 — Dotless kaf.** Isolated and final kaf carry a small internal stroke;
  the initial/medial forms are already strokeless. ss01 swaps only the isolated
  (`uni0643` → `kafDotless_ar`) and final (`uniFEDA` → `kafDotless_ar.fina`)
  forms to their dotless variants.
- **zero — Slashed zero.**

## Italic

The Italic is generated from the (corrected) Regular by `scripts/make-italic.py`,
which shears it by the italic angle (10°). This keeps a single source of truth:
every correction to the Regular flows into the Italic automatically, and the
lowercase `a` is simply the roman `a` slanted (the original bespoke italic `a`
was inconsistent, and history confirms there was never a single-story form).
Courier Prime's italic is itself essentially an oblique, so this matches the
original closely. Genuine cursive forms that differ from a plain slant live in
`sources/italic-overrides/` and are grafted on after shearing — currently just
the descending `f`; drop a hand-drawn `.glif` there (e.g. a single-story `a`) to
add more. Regenerate with `make italic`. A STAT table (`scripts/add-stat.py`,
post-build) declares the `ital` axis linking Roman↔Italic.

## Variable font

The **shipping** family is a clean **wght + ital** design
(`sources/CourierBadi.designspace`, four masters). gftools splits italic into
its own file (the GF convention), producing `CourierBadi[wght].ttf` and
`CourierBadi-Italic[wght].ttf`. This is the Google-Fonts submission and passes
the googlefonts Fontbakery profile with **0 errors / 0 failures** — no
parametric-axis or family-packaging issues. `make build` builds it.

### Contrast (XOPQ) axis — bonus build

The experimental contrast axis lives in a **separate** designspace
(`sources/CourierBadi-Contrast.designspace`, config `sources/contrast.yaml`,
built by `make contrast-vf` into `fonts-contrast/`). It adds the registered
parametric **XOPQ** ("vertical stroke thickness") axis. It is shipped only as a
release extra and previewed by `documentation/contrast-specimen.html`
(`scripts/make-contrast-specimen.py`), kept out of the main build so the GF
checks' known crashes on parametric axes never touch the clean submission.

The axis needs a **contrast sibling at every weight**, not just Bold — otherwise
the XOPQ endpoints at regular weight both alias the plain Regular and the axis
does nothing until Bold. `scripts/make-contrast-master.py` builds each sibling
as a **weight-neutral** modulation of its base master (thicken verticals `+dx`,
thin horizontals `-dy`), so XOPQ reads as contrast (orthogonal to wght) at any
weight. All masters share one point structure, so they interpolate; the winding
normalization above is what makes that possible across scripts. `make masters`
regenerates every master (base weights + contrast siblings).

## Contour winding

The Latin was digitized with the PostScript convention (outer CCW, holes CW),
but the merged Cyrillic/Greek/Arabic — and some individual glyphs like the
Vietnamese precomposed letters — came in wound the other way, sometimes only in
part (base backwards, accents correct). Inconsistent winding silently broke the
emboldening (which offsets along the contour normal), thinning those glyphs
instead of thickening them. `scripts/normalize-winding.py` resolves each glyph's
true filled region with skia-pathops (`simplify`) and rewrites it with canonical
winding (converting the result back to cubics), which also merges overlaps. Run
it on the Regular before regenerating the other masters.

`f` is **excluded** (`SKIP` in the script): it has an interpolation partner — the
descending italic `f` in `sources/italic-overrides/` — that shares its
two-contour structure. `simplify()` would merge those two contours into one and
break the match, so the roman `f` keeps its original (already-correct, native
Latin) winding untouched, and roman ↔ descending-italic `f` interpolate.

## Bold (emboldening)

The Bold is generated from the Regular by `scripts/make-bold.py`, which dilates
the outlines: every point is offset outward along the right-hand normal of its
travel direction, which grows the outer contours and shrinks the counters, so
strokes thicken while the advance width (and thus the strict monospace) is
unchanged. Because the width can't change, this weight axis behaves like a GRAD
(grade): bolding never reflows text. Bold Italic is the emboldened Italic. The
Regular and Bold are interpolation-compatible (point structure is preserved), so
they are the two masters of the `wght` axis. Regenerate with `make masters`.

Anchors move with the dilation (`move_anchors`): an anchor above a glyph's
ink goes up by the offset, one below goes down, one beside it goes out
sideways, and one inside the ink (a dagesh centre, a dot's own anchor) stays.
A mark's attaching anchor sits on its ink edge and so moves with it, which
keeps the Regular's clearance between marks and letters in the Bold. The
Hebrew presentation-form composites are re-placed from the moved anchors. The
contrast siblings (`make-contrast-master.py`) use the same rule with their own
offsets. (The fallback-reproducing anchors of `make-mark-anchors.py` are
measured on each master's ink afterwards anyway.)

## seen/sheen/sad teeth widening

`scripts/widen-seen-family.py` spreads the scrunched teeth of the seen/sheen/sad
family so they fill the cell, to the **same teeth width in every positional
form** and always inside the box. Isolated/final forms spread the teeth left and
let the free terminal tail smuggle out past the left edge; initial/medial forms
stretch only the teeth *cluster* and compress the baseline connector(s), which
stay pinned to the cell edge so joins line up and nothing overhangs into the
next letter. An x-direction inset thins the fattened vertical stems back to a
constant, monolinear stroke; the dots keep their shape and are only
repositioned. The family is derived from Unicode letter names, so every dotted
variant (two-dot, four-dot, …) is covered. Run once from pristine glyphs.

The stem inset must **not** touch the baseline join connectors: a point that
sits on a cell edge (x = 0 or x = 1228) is a connector to the neighbouring
letter and is pinned in place, because insetting it would pull the join stroke
inward and leave a visible gap between letters. (This was a real bug — medial and
final forms ended up ~26 units short of the edge and didn't quite touch.)

## Cyrillic diacritic centering

The Cyrillic merged from Novikov's fork stored accented letters as standalone
outlines, several with the top mark shifted off-center. `scripts/
center-cyrillic-marks.py` recenters the safe cases (symmetric marks —
breve/dieresis/macron/double-acute — over non-dotted bases). It is idempotent
and can be re-run if Cyrillic is re-imported.

## Hebrew

The Hebrew block (letters U+05D0–05EA, Yiddish ligatures U+05F0–05F2,
punctuation, all points and cantillation marks U+0591–05C7, and the
presentation forms U+FB1D–FB4F) is adapted from **Cousine** (SIL OFL 1.1;
Copyright 2026 The Cousine Project Authors, https://github.com/googlefonts/cousine;
designer Steve Matteson per its name table; its Hebrew outlines measure
identical to Liberation Mono 2.1.5's).
It is a monoline monospace already, so it needs fitting, not redrawing. As
everywhere else, only the Regular is touched; the other masters are derived.

1. `scripts/import-hebrew.py --donor Cousine-Regular.ttf` imports the raw
   outlines (cubic, canonical winding via skia-pathops) and turns the donor's
   mark positioning into named anchors: one class per donor mark lookup
   (`bottom`, `top`, `topleft`, `topright`, `bottomright`, `holam`, `center`,
   `shindot`, `sindot`, `lowerdot`, `upperdot`). Where the donor positions a
   mark by composing into a presentation form instead (dagesh, shin/sin dot,
   holam on vav, rafe), the base's anchor comes from that form's component
   offset. The presentation forms themselves become composites (base + mark
   components); the "wide" letters FB21–FB28 are plain references to their
   letter, since a monospace cannot widen them.
2. `scripts/fit-hebrew.py` fits it: y × 0.888 (he = 1086, between x-height and
   cap height), x × 0.93 around the cell centre (so alef/shin keep a side
   bearing like W), then convex corners rounded like the Latin terminals, then
   a normal inset that brings the stems to the Latin's **150 / 144** (measured
   from the scaled donor: vav 172 → 150, he roof 151 → 144). Marks keep
   advance 0, their stroke parts get the same inset, dots keep their size; top
   and bottom marks get mark-to-mark anchors so cantillation stacks. The
   composites' mark components are placed from the anchors. `--check` verifies
   the stems, the cell, the advances and the composites numerically.
3. `scripts/normalize-winding.py --glyphs …` (the Hebrew outlines), then
   `scripts/make-mark-anchors.py`, `make arabic-features` (adds
   `languagesystem hebr dflt` and the GDEF classes) and `make masters`.

Mark placement follows the donor (Liberation Mono): vowels under final kaf
and final nun sit beside the descender rather than inside the letter, holam
sits at the upper left, the shuruq dot left of vav. One exception: the holam
male sits above-left of the vav's head, its ink ending at the stem's line (the
donor puts it straight over the stem, where it reads as part of the vav; the
Culmus fonts, DejaVu Sans and Noto Serif Hebrew all put it to the left), and
U+05BA (holam haser for vav, consonantal vav + holam) 60 units further left,
so the two stay distinct. And the dagesh in yod sits beside the yod, left of
it at its height, as the donor's own precomposed U+FB39 draws it (Culmus
keeps it within the yod's height too); the donor's GPOS puts it under the
yod, where it reads as "!" (`fit-hebrew.py`, `yod_dagesh`).

**Meteg and below accents beside a vowel.** After a below vowel on the same
letter, meteg or a below cantillation mark goes beside the vowel at its level
instead of under it, as the donor does with contextual lookups. Here it is
done with ufo2ft **contextual anchors** (`fit-hebrew.py`, `side_by_side`), so
the GPOS comes from per-master anchors and is right in every master; fea
written by hand would carry the Regular's coordinates into Bold and Italic.
Those marks attach through their own class `bottomacc`; each letter has a
contextual anchor `*bottomacc.vN` per vowel-width group (context "* [vowels]
&") and `*bottom.next` (context "* & [marks]"), so the vowel moves right and
the mark left, separated by half the vowel's width + 60 + half the median mark
width; a mark filtering set skips dagesh, shin dots and top marks. Compared
with the donor's own shaping on the same strings (205 letter + vowel + mark
sequences, the donor scaled to these metrics), the level matches (within 19
units) and the separation is narrower than the donor's fixed one: by 46–251
units (median 151), since here it follows the vowel's width.

**Without GPOS** (Lexington's PDF writer, other renderers that do no
shaping) the marks still overlay their letter: right-to-left, a zero-width
mark is drawn from the letter's own origin, at its default outline position.
The donor drew every point centred on the cell, which made the shin and sin
dots look alike and put the holam male on the vav's stem.
`scripts/place-default-marks.py` (run by `make masters` on every
master, measured on that master's own ink) moves those marks' outlines and
their own anchors together, so the GPOS still places them as before:

- shin dot U+05C1 over shin's right arm, sin dot U+05C2 over its left arm
  (their GPOS place on shin; the Culmus fonts and DejaVu Sans draw them so);
- holam U+05B9 at the holam male's place, above-left of the vav's head; on
  other letters this is left of the centre, nearer a holam haser's top-left
  than the old centred dot; U+05BA at its own place on vav;
- the dagesh U+05BC stays centred, which is right for most letters; in vav
  (shuruq), zayin, yod and final nun it hides in the stem. One glyph cannot
  be placed for all of them (a dot left of vav's stem sits on tav's left
  leg), so a renderer without GPOS should print the presentation forms
  there: U+FB35 is vav with dagesh, drawn as a shuruq. Lexington's PDF
  writer composes a pointed letter into its form when the font has one.

The presentation forms (U+FB2A shin with shin dot, U+FB2B with sin dot, U+FB35
vav with dagesh, U+FB4B vav with holam, …) are placed again from the anchors
afterwards, so a renderer that uses them gets the GPOS placement.
`scripts/check-mark-parity.py --hebrew-against OLD/ fonts/ttf/*.ttf` compares
a build's Hebrew GPOS placement with an earlier build's.

The dagesh U+05BC is drawn 184 units across (the vowel points stay 148, the
stroke's width): at 12 pt the 148-unit dot was 0.3 mm and all but vanished
in yod (beside it) and lamed (in its open bowl). It is scaled about its own
anchor, so every placement is kept; in yod it sits 72 units left of the
stem (was 88), so it is read with the yod. Its tightest fit is in shin
(35 units from the arms).

U+05EF (yod triangle), which the donor does not have, is built by
`fit-hebrew.py` from the fitted yod: three yods scaled 0.9 (one on top, two
below) as outlines, with the stroke restored to 150 / 144 by the same normal
offset as the fit (scaled components would have 135-unit stems).
